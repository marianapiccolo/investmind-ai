# Portfolio Diversification

Diversification is the distribution of investment exposure across different
assets, issuers, sectors, geographic regions, or other sources of risk.

The purpose of diversification is to reduce dependence on a limited number of
positions or risk factors.

Diversification can reduce certain portfolio risks, but it cannot eliminate
all investment risk or prevent losses during broad market declines.

## Concentration Risk

Concentration risk occurs when a large portion of the portfolio depends on a
small number of investments or closely related exposures.

A portfolio may appear to contain many positions while still being
concentrated if those positions share similar economic drivers.

Position weights are therefore useful when evaluating concentration.

A very large individual position can have a disproportionate effect on total
portfolio performance and risk.

There is no universal position-weight threshold that defines an adequately
diversified portfolio.

## Asset Allocation

Asset allocation describes how portfolio capital is distributed among asset
classes or investment categories.

Examples may include equities, bonds, funds, cash-equivalent instruments, or
other assets.

Different asset classes can respond differently to economic and market
conditions.

Asset allocation therefore influences both expected portfolio behavior and
the types of risks to which the portfolio is exposed.

## Correlation

Correlation describes the degree to which two return series have historically
moved together.

Positive correlation means two assets have tended to move in the same
direction.

Negative correlation means they have tended to move in opposite directions.

Correlation close to zero indicates a weaker linear relationship.

Combining assets with imperfect correlations can improve diversification
because their returns may not move identically at the same time.

Historical correlations are not stable constants and can change significantly
during periods of market stress.

## Number of Holdings

The number of holdings alone does not determine diversification quality.

A portfolio containing many highly correlated securities may provide less
diversification than a smaller portfolio containing exposures with different
risk drivers.

Portfolio weights, sectors, asset types, and correlations should therefore be
considered together.

## Diversification Limitations

Diversification primarily addresses risks that can be reduced by spreading
exposure.

It cannot remove broad systematic risks that affect large portions of the
financial market.

Diversification analysis is therefore one component of portfolio risk
analysis rather than a guarantee against losses.

# Portfolio Risk

Portfolio risk describes the uncertainty surrounding the future value and
returns of an investment portfolio.

Risk is multidimensional. A portfolio should not be evaluated using a single
metric in isolation. Volatility, drawdown, concentration, correlations, asset
allocation, liquidity, and investment horizon can all contribute to the risk
profile.

## Volatility

Volatility measures how much portfolio returns fluctuate over time.

Higher volatility means returns have historically varied more widely around
their average. Lower volatility means returns have been more stable.

Volatility measures variability, not whether returns are good or bad. A
portfolio can have low volatility and still lose money, and a portfolio can
have high volatility while producing positive long-term returns.

Historical volatility does not guarantee future volatility.

## Maximum Drawdown

Maximum drawdown measures the largest observed decline from a portfolio peak
to a subsequent trough during the analyzed period.

For example, if a portfolio reaches 100, falls to 75, and later recovers, the
peak-to-trough decline is 25%.

Drawdown helps describe the magnitude of historical losses and can provide a
different perspective from volatility.

A large drawdown may be important because an investor must recover from the
loss before reaching the previous portfolio peak again.

Drawdown depends heavily on the period analyzed and should not be interpreted
as a prediction of the largest possible future loss.

## Downside Risk

Traditional volatility treats positive and negative return deviations
symmetrically.

Downside-oriented measures focus specifically on unfavorable outcomes, such
as negative returns or returns below a chosen threshold.

This distinction can be useful because investors are usually more concerned
about losses than unusually high positive returns.

## Risk and Time Horizon

The relevance of portfolio risk depends partly on the investor's time horizon.

Short-term portfolio fluctuations may be more important when money will be
needed soon. A longer investment horizon may provide more time to recover from
market declines, but it does not eliminate investment risk.

Risk metrics should therefore be interpreted together with the investment
objective, liquidity needs, and time horizon.
